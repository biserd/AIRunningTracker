# Product design principles

These are required review criteria for every feature, new screen, redesign and experiment. They apply to first-time and returning users on every supported platform. A visual refresh alone is not a pass.

1. **One job, one clear action.** Name the user’s task and make the next useful action obvious. Use familiar native controls and words users understand. Keep back, cancel and accessibility actions reachable; put advanced options where they are needed. Prefer removing redundant controls, cards and copy to adding instructions.
2. **Shortest safe path.** Count required taps, surfaces and decisions before and after from the same starting state. Include OS and provider sheets separately. Infer safe defaults, request the least necessary input, and avoid confirmations for reversible actions. Keep confirmations for charges, irreversible changes, consent, and high-impact writes. Explain any remaining steps.
3. **Value first.** Aim for a meaningful result in 60 seconds where feasible. Define the result and timer explicitly; opening a screen, tapping a CTA or starting a timer does not prove value. Let people try a real free action or a clearly labeled local sample before signup where safe. Do not weaken auth, consent or data ownership. Request permissions at the action that uses them.
4. **Findable, adaptive navigation.** Make current location, primary action and return path clear. Give frequent tasks a direct path. Preserve selection, scroll or draft where applicable across navigation, rotation and resize. Design for the available window, Dynamic Type, dark/light appearance, VoiceOver, keyboard, pointer and Reduce Motion.
5. **Honest subscription decisions.** Explain the outcome, what stays free, and what payment adds. Show the total amount charged, currency, interval, renewal terms and exact applicable trial duration and post-trial charge before confirmation. Use store/provider data and verified eligibility, not hardcoded offer copy. Use one purchase CTA, accessible restore and management, terms and privacy, and a visible dismissal when free use is available. Never preselect a charge, fabricate scarcity, hide a billing amount, or advertise an unknown trial.
6. **Upsell in context, after useful work when possible.** Offer a relevant benefit on voluntary paid-feature discovery, a real limit, or a runner-requested deeper result. Keep free tasks moving and never show a recurring launch or onboarding interruption. Label examples as examples, and only personalize from current, permitted inputs.
7. **Measure results, protect data.** Define the denominator, timeframe, deduplication, source of record, and guardrails before enabling measurement. Separate client intents from server/provider-confirmed results. Exclude private inputs, precise activity or health data, and direct identifiers from product analytics; respect consent and opt-outs. Experiments need an explicit decision, assignment unit, holdout, guardrails and rollback. Do not claim improvement without comparable evidence.

## Review gate

- [ ] State the screen’s job, primary action, free path, and shortest return path for a new and a returning user.
- [ ] Document before/after taps, screens and decisions, first meaningful result, and justified nonremovable friction.
- [ ] Verify happy, empty, loading, offline/error, canceled, pending, successful and returning states as applicable. Verify disabled actions explain what to do next.
- [ ] For monetization, check contextual timing, dismissibility, exact localized billing, eligibility, restore/recovery, management and established entitlement handling. Never test with a live charge without explicit approval.
- [ ] Check native phone and tablet at representative widths, light and dark, large type, contrast, named controls and appropriate target sizes. Record actual simulator/device results. Manual checks such as VoiceOver or real multitasking must be labeled as unverified until run.
- [ ] Record verified results, hypotheses, scope, exceptions and owner. Exceptions need a user-centered reason (for example, statutory or provider consent) and a review date.

For native Apple work follow [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/) and [Apple subscription guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/). Keep platform guidance separate from app-specific decisions.
